Stay Informed. Stay Safe.

Landslides can happen with little warning, especially after prolonged or heavy rainfall. The Myanmar Landslide Division helps communities understand landslide hazards, explore risk information, and learn practical ways to prepare. LANDSLIDE SAFETY GUIDE

Know What to Do Before, During and After a Landslide

Preparation can reduce injuries and help families respond quickly. Landslides may happen suddenly, and not every event gives clear warning. Never wait for every warning sign if you feel unsafe or authorities advise evacuation.

Remember

Follow instructions from local authorities. This page provides general education, not a live warning service or a replacement for emergency instructions.

Recognise possible warning signs

New cracks

Watch for new or widening cracks in soil, roads, walls, foundations or paved areas. Ground may bulge or pull away from structures.

Leaning objects

Trees, fences, retaining walls and utility poles that suddenly tilt or move may indicate ground movement.

Unusual water flow

New seepage, sudden changes in stream level, muddy water or water appearing where it normally does not can be warning signs.

Changes to buildings

Doors or windows that suddenly stick, new wall cracks, or gaps between a building and the ground may signal movement.

Unusual sounds

Cracking trees, knocking rocks, rumbling or ground vibrations may mean debris is moving nearby.

Heavy or prolonged rain

Long periods of rain can saturate soil and increase the chance of slope failure, especially on steep or unstable slopes.

Before a landslide

Make a household plan
  • Find out whether your home, school or workplace is near a steep slope, drainage channel or area with past landslides.
  • Choose a safer place to meet and agree on how family members will contact one another.
  • Identify more than one route away from slopes and valleys because roads may become blocked.
  • Know the location of nearby shelters and follow local evacuation plans.
  • Keep important documents in a waterproof bag and store digital copies securely.
  • Help children, older adults, people with disabilities and pets plan how to leave safely.
Prepare an emergency bag
  • Drinking water and ready-to-eat food.
  • First-aid supplies and necessary medicines.
  • Flashlight, spare batteries and a battery-powered radio.
  • Phone, power bank and charging cable.
  • Whistle, basic hygiene items and a change of clothes.
  • Copies of identification, emergency contacts and some cash.

During a landslide or when danger is immediate

Move away from the path

If you see or hear a landslide and can move safely, get away from its path. Move toward safer ground and avoid valleys, drainage channels and the direction debris is travelling.

Follow evacuation instructions

Leave early when authorities advise evacuation. Do not cross moving mud, floodwater or debris, and do not stop to collect belongings.

If escape is not possible

If debris is striking the building you are in and escape is unsafe, move to the highest floor or a sturdy elevated position and protect your head. Call for help when safe.

After a landslide

Stay away from danger
  • Do not return until authorities say it is safe.
  • Stay away from the slide area because more movement may occur.
  • Watch for flooding, damaged roads and unstable buildings.
  • Avoid fallen power lines and report them to the relevant authorities.
Help safely
  • Call local emergency services and report missing or injured people.
  • Help others only if you can do so without entering the affected area.
  • Use text messages when phone networks are busy.
  • Do not enter damaged buildings or attempt repairs near unstable slopes.

Local preparedness in Myanmar

During the monsoon season, communities in hilly and mountainous areas should pay attention to prolonged rainfall, local announcements and changes around slopes. Families should discuss evacuation routes before severe weather begins and check on neighbours who may need assistance.

Do not rely on this educational website for real-time alerts. Follow announcements from responsible local authorities and emergency organisations.
