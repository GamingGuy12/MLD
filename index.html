Frequently Asked Questions

The Myanmar Landslide Division (M.L.D.) is an educational project website focused on landslide awareness and risk reduction in Myanmar. It brings together information about landslide hazards, at-risk areas, monitoring indicators, and practical safety measures. It is a student-developed demonstration and is not an official government emergency-warning service.

Open the interactive map to explore locations identified as landslide-prone. Treat the map as an educational reference rather than a live prediction: local risk can change with rainfall, ground conditions, slope stability, and construction. Follow official local warnings when making safety decisions.

Landslides can be triggered by intense or prolonged rainfall, earthquakes, steep or unstable slopes, erosion, deforestation, and construction that changes how water drains through the ground. These factors can weaken the soil or rock and cause it to move downhill.

Learn the warning signs, such as new cracks in the ground or walls, leaning trees or poles, unusual water seepage, and rumbling sounds. Prepare an evacuation route, keep an emergency bag ready, and move away from slopes, river channels, and other danger areas when authorities advise evacuation. Never wait to investigate a suspected landslide.

Move to a safe location away from the landslide path and follow instructions from local authorities and emergency responders. Do not return to the affected area until it has been declared safe, because additional slope movement may occur. Contact local emergency services or community response teams for assistance.
